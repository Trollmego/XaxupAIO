ITIL 4 Foundation Presentation (Slide-Ready Outline)
====================================================

- Each section is one slide.
- Copy slide titles and bullets into your deck tool.
- "Visual" suggests a graphic to place.
- "Notes" are optional speaker notes.

Slide 03 - Learning Objectives
------------------------------
- Define service, value, outcomes, costs, and risks
- Explain the Service Value System (SVS)
- Describe the Service Value Chain activities
- Apply the seven guiding principles
- Recognize key practices at foundation level
Visual: Target or checklist.
Notes:
- Align to exam outcomes.

Slide 04 - Why ITIL 4
--------------------
- Business demands speed and stability
- Digital products require ongoing services
- Integration with Agile, DevOps, Lean
- Focus on co-creating value with customers
Visual: Balance scale (speed vs stability).
Notes:
- Position ITIL 4 as a modern operating model.

Slide 05 - ITIL 4 Evolution (v3 to 4)
-------------------------------------
- v3: Process-focused lifecycle
- v4: Value-focused, flexible system
- Emphasis on value streams, practices, and culture
- Better alignment with modern delivery methods
Visual: Timeline or before/after graphic.
Notes:
- Avoid "replace" language; ITIL 4 builds on ITIL v3.

Slide 06 - Key Definitions
--------------------------
- Service: Enables value by facilitating outcomes
- Value: Benefits, usefulness, importance
- Outcome: Result for stakeholder
- Cost: Money, time, effort spent
- Risk: Uncertainty that can affect outcomes
Visual: Five icons with labels.
Notes:
- These definitions appear directly in the exam.

Slide 07 - Service Relationship Roles
-------------------------------------
- Service provider: delivers services
- Service consumer: uses services
- Service sponsor: funds services
- Customer vs user: customer defines requirements
Visual: Triangle with roles around a service.
Notes:
- Stress the difference between customer and user.

Slide 08 - Service Management
-----------------------------
Definition: Specialized organizational capabilities
to enable value for customers in the form of services.
- Focus on people, process, partners, technology
Visual: Capability wheel.
Notes:
- Emphasize capabilities, not just processes.

Slide 09 - Service Value System (SVS) Overview
----------------------------------------------
Inputs (demand, opportunities) -> SVS -> Value
- Unified model for how all components work together
Visual: SVS diagram (input -> SVS -> value).
Notes:
- This is the core model of ITIL 4.

Slide 10 - SVS Components
-------------------------
- Guiding principles
- Governance
- Service Value Chain
- Practices
- Continual improvement
Visual: Hub and spokes.
Notes:
- Explain that practices support value chain activities.

Slide 11 - Guiding Principles (List)
------------------------------------
1) Focus on value
2) Start where you are
3) Progress iteratively with feedback
4) Collaborate and promote visibility
5) Think and work holistically
6) Keep it simple and practical
7) Optimize and automate
Visual: Seven icons or tiles.
Notes:
- These apply to any initiative, any level.

Slide 12 - Guiding Principles in Action
---------------------------------------
- Focus on value: measure outcomes, not activity
- Start where you are: baseline current state
- Iterate with feedback: short cycles, learn fast
- Collaborate: reduce silos, share work in progress
- Keep it simple: remove steps that add no value
Visual: Principle-to-example mapping table.
Notes:
- Show practical examples to anchor memory.

Slide 13 - Service Value Chain Activities
-----------------------------------------
- Plan
- Improve
- Engage
- Design and transition
- Obtain or build
- Deliver and support
Visual: Circular chain with six activities.
Notes:
- Any value stream uses a subset of these activities.

Slide 14 - Value Chain Example (New SaaS Feature)
-------------------------------------------------
Demand -> Engage -> Plan -> Design/Transition -> Obtain/Build
-> Deliver/Support -> Feedback -> Improve
Visual: Flow with feedback loop.
Notes:
- Use a real example the audience can relate to.

Slide 15 - Governance
---------------------
- Aligns direction and policy with strategy
- Evaluates, directs, and monitors the SVS
- Ensures compliance and accountability
Visual: Board/steering committee icon.
Notes:
- Governance is not daily operations.

Slide 16 - Continual Improvement Model
--------------------------------------
1) What is the vision?
2) Where are we now?
3) Where do we want to be?
4) How do we get there?
5) Take action
6) Did we get there?
7) How do we keep the momentum?
Visual: Improvement cycle.
Notes:
- Apply at all levels: service, product, org.

Slide 17 - Four Dimensions Overview
-----------------------------------
- Organizations and people
- Information and technology
- Partners and suppliers
- Value streams and processes
Visual: Four-quadrant diagram.
Notes:
- All dimensions must be balanced for good service.

Slide 18 - Dimension 1: Organizations and People
------------------------------------------------
- Roles, responsibilities, and culture
- Skills, staffing, and organizational structure
- Leadership and communication
Visual: Org chart or people icons.
Notes:
- Culture drives adoption more than tools.

Slide 19 - Dimension 2: Information and Technology
--------------------------------------------------
- Data quality, knowledge, and information flow
- Tools, automation, and architecture
- Security, privacy, and compliance
Visual: Data pipeline or technology stack.
Notes:
- Highlight that information is as important as tools.

Slide 20 - Dimension 3: Partners and Suppliers
----------------------------------------------
- Sourcing strategies and contracts
- Integration with third parties
- Shared risk and performance
Visual: Handshake and network icons.
Notes:
- Services often depend on external providers.

Slide 21 - Dimension 4: Value Streams and Processes
---------------------------------------------------
- End-to-end flow from demand to value
- Clear handoffs, visibility, and feedback
- Process models support value streams
Visual: Stream or conveyor belt.
Notes:
- Shift from siloed processes to flow.

Slide 22 - Practices Overview
-----------------------------
- ITIL 4 has 34 practices
- Three categories:
  - General management
  - Service management
  - Technical management
Visual: 3-column grid.
Notes:
- Practices replace the old process-only view.

Slide 23 - Key Practices to Know (Foundation)
---------------------------------------------
- Incident management
- Problem management
- Change enablement
- Service request management
- Service desk
- Service level management
- Monitoring and event management
- Service configuration management
- IT asset management
- Continual improvement
Visual: Highlighted list.
Notes:
- Focus on purpose and value of each practice.

Slide 24 - Incident Management (Purpose)
----------------------------------------
- Restore normal service quickly
- Minimize business impact
- Use prioritization and escalation
Key outputs: resolved incidents, workarounds
Visual: Alert -> response -> restore.
Notes:
- Incident and service request are different.

Slide 25 - Change Enablement (Purpose)
--------------------------------------
- Maximize successful changes
- Assess risk and optimize speed
- Balance stability and agility
Types: standard, normal, emergency
Visual: Risk gauge with change types.
Notes:
- Replace "change management" term in ITIL 4.

Slide 26 - Service Request + Service Desk
-----------------------------------------
Service Request:
- Handle pre-defined, low-risk user needs
Service Desk:
- Single point of contact for users
Visual: Portal + help desk.
Notes:
- Service desk supports both incidents and requests.

Slide 27 - Problem Management + Monitoring/Event
------------------------------------------------
Problem Management:
- Identify root causes, prevent recurrence
Monitoring and Event:
- Detect, classify, and respond to events
Visual: Root cause tree + monitoring dashboard.
Notes:
- Event management supports proactive detection.

Slide 28 - Service Level Management
-----------------------------------
- Set clear targets and measure performance
- Align expectations with business outcomes
- Use SLAs, OLAs, and underpinning contracts
Visual: KPI dashboard.
Notes:
- Talk about usefulness, not just uptime.

Slide 29 - Service Configuration + IT Asset
-------------------------------------------
- Configuration items and relationships
- Accurate data supports impact analysis
- Asset lifecycle management and compliance
Visual: CMDB graph.
Notes:
- Data quality is critical for trust.

Slide 30 - Metrics and Measurement
----------------------------------
- Outcomes: value delivered
- Outputs: work completed
- KPIs and critical success factors (CSFs)
Examples: MTTR, change success rate, SLA attainment
Visual: Metrics dashboard.
Notes:
- Measure what matters to the customer.

Slide 31 - Implementation and Adoption
--------------------------------------
- Start small and scale
- Build a prioritized improvement backlog
- Engage stakeholders early
- Automate only after stabilizing the process
Visual: Roadmap with milestones.
Notes:
- Tie improvements to business outcomes.

Slide 32 - Common Pitfalls
--------------------------
- Focusing on tools over people and process
- Optimizing a single team instead of the flow
- Skipping feedback loops
- Over-engineering procedures
Visual: Warning signs.
Notes:
- Use this slide for practical advice.

Slide 33 - Exam Overview and Tips
---------------------------------
- 40 multiple-choice questions, 60 minutes
- Pass mark: 26/40 (65 percent)
- Closed book, no negative marking
Tips:
- Memorize definitions and key models
- Use elimination and time-boxing
Visual: Exam checklist.
Notes:
- Encourage practice questions.
