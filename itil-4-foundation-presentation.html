What is ITIL?

ITIL Definition

ITIL (Information Technology Infrastructure Library) is the most widely adopted framework for IT Service Management (ITSM) in the world. It provides a practical and flexible approach to support organizations on their journey to digital transformation.

🌍
Global Standard
Adopted by millions of professionals worldwide across all industries and sectors
📚
Best Practices
Collected from real-world experience and refined over 30+ years
🔄
Flexible Framework
Adaptable to any organization size, industry, or technology environment
💼
Business Aligned
Focuses on delivering value and meeting business objectives

ITIL 4 vs ITIL v3

Aspect ITIL v3 (2011) ITIL 4 (2019)
Core Structure Service Lifecycle (5 stages) Service Value System (SVS)
Processes 26 Processes 34 Practices
Focus Process-centric Value co-creation & holistic
Methodology Waterfall-friendly Agile, DevOps, Lean integrated
Dimensions 4 Ps (People, Process, Products, Partners) Four Dimensions of Service Management
Guiding Concepts 9 Guiding Principles 7 Guiding Principles
💡 Key Evolution

ITIL 4 shifts from "processes" to "practices" to emphasize flexibility. Practices include not just process activities, but also people, technology, and information needed to deliver value.

Key Concepts of Service Management

Core Definitions

Service

A means of enabling value co-creation by facilitating outcomes that customers want to achieve, without the customer having to manage specific costs and risks.

Service Management

A set of specialized organizational capabilities for enabling value for customers in the form of services.

Value

The perceived benefits, usefulness, and importance of something. Value is always co-created through active collaboration between providers and consumers.

🎯 Exam Focus

Remember: Value is co-created - it's not delivered TO customers, but created WITH them.

Organizations & Stakeholders

Organization

A person or group of people that has its own functions with responsibilities, authorities, and relationships to achieve its objectives.

Service Provider

An organization that delivers services to consumers. Can be internal (IT department) or external (third-party vendor).

Service Consumer

An organization that receives services. Includes three roles:

  • Customer - Defines requirements, accountable for outcomes
  • User - Uses the services
  • Sponsor - Authorizes budget
Important: A single person can take on multiple roles. For example, an employee might be the customer, user, AND sponsor for a software subscription.

Products, Services & Service Offerings

Product

A configuration of an organization's resources designed to offer value for a consumer.

Service Offering

A formal description of one or more services designed to address the needs of a target consumer group. It includes:

📦
Goods
Physical items transferred to the consumer (e.g., a laptop, phone, server)
🔐
Access to Resources
Permission to use resources under agreed terms (e.g., cloud storage, network access)
🎯
Service Actions
Actions performed to address consumer needs (e.g., user support, training)
🎯 Remember

Service offerings combine goods, access to resources, and service actions to meet consumer needs.

The Four Dimensions of Service Management

All four dimensions must be considered for effective service management. Neglecting any dimension can result in services that don't meet expectations.

👥 1. Organizations & People
  • Formal organizational structure
  • Culture and mindset
  • Staffing and competencies
  • Roles and responsibilities
  • Communication and collaboration
📊 2. Information & Technology
  • Information management
  • Knowledge and data
  • Technologies and tools
  • Automation capabilities
  • AI and machine learning
🤝 3. Partners & Suppliers
  • Supplier relationships
  • Contracts and agreements
  • Service integration
  • Outsourcing strategies
  • Vendor management
🔄 4. Value Streams & Processes
  • Activities and workflows
  • Process optimization
  • Value stream mapping
  • Automation of processes
  • Continuous improvement
External Factors (PESTLE): Political, Economic, Social, Technological, Legal, Environmental factors affect all four dimensions.

ITIL Service Value System (SVS)

What is the SVS?

The Service Value System describes how all components and activities of an organization work together as a system to enable value creation. It ensures the organization continually co-creates value with stakeholders.

The SVS takes opportunity/demand as input and transforms it into value as output through its five components.

SVS Components

🧭

Recommendations that guide an organization in all circumstances, regardless of changes in goals, strategies, type of work, or management structure.

The means by which an organization is directed and controlled. Ensures policies, direction, and oversight are in place.
⛓️

📈

A recurring organizational activity performed at all levels to ensure performance meets stakeholder expectations. Applies across the entire SVS.

The 7 Guiding Principles

What are Guiding Principles?

Recommendations that guide an organization in ALL circumstances, regardless of changes in its goals, strategies, type of work, or management structure. They are universal and enduring.

1. Focus on Value
2. Start Where You Are
3. Progress Iteratively with Feedback
4. Collaborate and Promote Visibility
5. Think and Work Holistically
6. Keep It Simple and Practical
7. Optimize and Automate
🎯 Exam Focus

You must know all 7 principles and understand how to apply them. Expect 2-4 exam questions on guiding principles.

Principle 1: Focus on Value

Everything the organization does should link back, directly or indirectly, to value for stakeholders.

Key Applications

  • Know your consumer: Understand who is being served and what they value
  • Understand the customer's perspective: See services from their point of view
  • Map value to outcomes: Link activities to desired business outcomes
  • Consider customer experience (CX): The entire journey matters, not just the endpoint
❓
Questions to Ask
• Why is the consumer using this service?
• What does success look like for them?
• How does this activity contribute to value?
⚠️
Common Mistakes
• Assuming you know what customers value
• Focusing on internal metrics only
• Ignoring the end-user experience

Principle 2: Start Where You Are

Do not start from scratch without first considering what is already available to be leveraged.

Key Applications

  • Assess current state objectively: Measure what exists before making changes
  • Leverage existing services: Don't reinvent the wheel unnecessarily
  • Apply risk management: Understand what you're changing from
  • Build on what works: Recognize and preserve successful elements
✓ Do

• Conduct thorough assessments
• Use direct observation
• Measure actual performance
• Identify what's working well

✗ Avoid

• Assuming everything is broken
• Relying only on reports/metrics
• Discarding all existing work
• Over-engineering solutions

Principle 3: Progress Iteratively with Feedback

Do not attempt to do everything at once. Organize work into smaller, manageable sections that can be executed and completed in a timely manner.

Plan
→
Do
→
Check
→
Act
↻

Key Applications

  • Use timeboxing: Set clear boundaries for iterations (sprints)
  • Seek feedback continuously: Get input before, during, and after each iteration
  • Make improvements in small steps: Reduce risk of large failures
  • Use feedback loops: Ensure learning flows back into the process
🎯 Agile Alignment

This principle aligns directly with Agile and DevOps methodologies - emphasizing short cycles, continuous feedback, and rapid iteration.

Principle 4: Collaborate and Promote Visibility

Working together across boundaries produces results with greater buy-in, more relevance to objectives, and increased likelihood of long-term success.

Collaborate

  • Involve the right stakeholders at the right time
  • Work across organizational boundaries
  • Include customers in service design
  • Build trust through transparency

Promote Visibility

  • Share information openly
  • Make work visible (Kanban boards)
  • Avoid hidden agendas
  • Communicate decisions and reasons
Why Visibility Matters

Poor visibility leads to poor decision-making. When work is hidden, people make assumptions, duplicate efforts, and create conflicting initiatives. Transparency enables better coordination and trust.

Principle 5: Think and Work Holistically

No service, practice, process, department, or supplier stands alone. The outcomes will suffer unless the organization works on the service as a whole.

Systems Thinking Approach

  • Recognize complexity: Services are complex systems with many interconnected parts
  • Understand dependencies: Changes in one area affect others
  • Consider end-to-end value: Look at the entire value stream
  • Avoid siloed thinking: Don't optimize one part at the expense of the whole
🔗
Connections Matter
Every component (people, technology, processes, partners) must work together. A failure in one area can cascade throughout the system.
🎯
Focus on Outcomes
Don't just optimize activities - focus on how they contribute to the overall desired outcome and value creation.

Principle 6: Keep It Simple and Practical

If a process, service, action, or metric provides no value or produces no useful outcome, eliminate it.

Key Applications

  • Use minimum viable approach: Start with what's essential and add only when needed
  • Avoid over-engineering: Don't create complex solutions for simple problems
  • Question every step: Ask "Does this add value?" for each activity
  • Reduce unnecessary complexity: Simplify processes wherever possible
Fewer Steps = Better

Each step in a process is an opportunity for error, delay, and waste. Fewer steps mean faster delivery and fewer failure points.

Outcome-Based Thinking

Focus on producing practical, outcome-based solutions that deliver results, not on following complex procedures for their own sake.

💡 Remember

This principle supports Lean thinking - eliminate waste and focus only on value-adding activities.

Principle 7: Optimize and Automate

Resources of all types, particularly human resources, should be used to their best effect. Eliminate waste and use technology to achieve whatever it is capable of.

Optimize First

  • Understand and agree on the context
  • Assess the current state of optimization
  • Simplify and/or eliminate before automating
  • Use the other guiding principles to guide optimization

Then Automate

  • Standardize before automating
  • Focus on repetitive, high-volume tasks
  • Use technology appropriately
  • Monitor and measure automation outcomes
⚠️ Critical Sequence

OPTIMIZE before you AUTOMATE! Automating a broken or inefficient process just makes problems happen faster. Always simplify and optimize first, then automate.

The Service Value Chain

What is the Service Value Chain?

The central element of the SVS - an operating model that outlines the key activities required to respond to demand and facilitate value realization through the creation and management of products and services.

The 6 Value Chain Activities

Plan

Shared understanding of vision, status, and direction

Improve

Continual improvement across all activities

Engage

Stakeholder needs and transparency

Design & Transition

Meet specifications and expectations

Obtain/Build

Components available when needed

Deliver & Support

Services delivered per specifications

Key Point: These activities are interconnected, not linear. Different combinations create different value streams for various scenarios.

Value Chain Activities in Detail

📋 Plan

Ensures a shared understanding of the vision, current status, and improvement direction for all four dimensions and all products/services.

Key outputs: Strategic/tactical plans, portfolio decisions, policies

📈 Improve

Ensures continual improvement of products, services, and practices across all value chain activities and the four dimensions.

Key outputs: Improvement initiatives, improvement status reports

🤝 Engage

Provides a good understanding of stakeholder needs, transparency, and continual engagement with all stakeholders.

Key outputs: Prioritized requirements, incidents, service requests

🎨 Design & Transition

Ensures products and services continually meet stakeholder expectations for quality, costs, and time to market.

Key outputs: New/changed products and services

🔧 Obtain/Build

Ensures service components are available when and where they are needed and meet agreed specifications.

Key outputs: Service components, knowledge, contracts

🚀 Deliver & Support

Ensures services are delivered and supported according to agreed specifications and stakeholder expectations.

Key outputs: Services delivered, support provided

Incident Management

Purpose

To minimize the negative impact of incidents by restoring normal service operation as quickly as possible.

Incident

An unplanned interruption to a service, or reduction in the quality of a service.

Major Incident

An incident with significant business impact, requiring an immediate coordinated resolution.

Key Activities

  • Logging and categorization: Record all incidents with appropriate details
  • Prioritization: Based on urgency and impact
  • Diagnosis: Identify the nature of the incident
  • Resolution and recovery: Restore service as quickly as possible
  • Closure: Confirm resolution and user satisfaction
🎯 Key Distinction

Incident vs Problem: Incidents are about RESTORING service quickly. Problems are about finding the ROOT CAUSE to prevent future incidents.

Problem Management

Purpose

To reduce the likelihood and impact of incidents by identifying actual and potential causes of incidents, and managing workarounds and known errors.

❓
Problem
A cause, or potential cause, of one or more incidents
⚠️
Known Error
A problem that has been analyzed but not yet resolved
🔧
Workaround
A solution that reduces or eliminates impact when full resolution isn't available

Change Enablement

Purpose

To maximize the number of successful IT changes by ensuring that risks are properly assessed, authorizing changes to proceed, and managing the change schedule.

Change

The addition, modification, or removal of anything that could have a direct or indirect effect on services.

Types of Changes

Standard Change
Pre-authorized, low-risk, well-understood changes following a documented procedure

Example: Password reset

Normal Change
Must be scheduled, assessed, and authorized following the normal process

Example: Server upgrade

Emergency Change
Must be implemented ASAP to resolve an incident or security issue

Example: Security patch

🎯 Key Concept

Change Authority: A person or group responsible for authorizing a change. Different change types may have different authorities.

Service Desk

Purpose

To be the entry point and single point of contact for the service provider with all of its users. It captures demand for incident resolution and service requests.

Key Point: Service Desk is a practice, not a function. It represents a team and their capabilities for user communication and support.

Service Desk Types

🏢
Local
Located close to users it serves, providing personal support and local knowledge
🌍
Centralized
Single location serving all users, efficient but may lack local context
🌐
Virtual
Geographically distributed staff appear as single team through technology

Key Skills for Service Desk Staff

  • Empathy and emotional intelligence
  • Incident analysis and prioritization
  • Effective communication
  • Understanding of business priority

Service Level Management

Purpose

To set clear business-based targets for service levels, and to ensure that delivery of services is properly assessed, monitored, and managed against these targets.

Service Level Agreement (SLA)

A documented agreement between a service provider and a customer that identifies both services required and the expected level of service.

Service Level

One or more metrics that define expected or achieved service quality.

SLA Best Practices

  • Focus on outcomes: Measure what matters to the business, not just technical metrics
  • Use simple language: Avoid technical jargon in customer-facing SLAs
  • Be realistic: Set achievable targets that can be measured
  • Regular reviews: Conduct service reviews with customers
⚠️ Watermelon SLA Warning

Avoid "Watermelon SLAs" - green on the outside (metrics look good) but red inside (customers unhappy). Always validate SLA metrics against actual customer satisfaction.

Service Request Management

Purpose

To support the agreed quality of a service by handling all pre-defined, user-initiated service requests in an effective and user-friendly manner.

Service Request

A request from a user or user's authorized representative that initiates a service action agreed as part of service delivery.

Types of Service Requests

🔑
Access
Request for access to a service
ℹ️
Information
Request for information or advice
📦
Provision
Provision of a resource or service
💬
Feedback
Compliments, complaints, comments
🎯 Key Distinction

Service Request vs Incident: Service requests are NORMAL service delivery (expected). Incidents are UNPLANNED interruptions (unexpected).

Continual Improvement Practice

Purpose

To align the organization's practices and services with changing business needs through the ongoing improvement of products, services, and practices, or any element involved in management of products and services.

The Continual Improvement Model

1. What is the vision?
2. Where are we now?
3. Where do we want to be?
4. How do we get there?
5. Take action
6. Did we get there?
7. How do we keep the momentum going?

Information Security Management

Purpose

To protect the information needed by the organization to conduct its business. This includes understanding and managing risks to the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of information.

CIA Triad

🔒
Confidentiality
Information is not made available or disclosed to unauthorized entities
✓
Integrity
Information is accurate and complete, protected from unauthorized modification
⚡
Availability
Information is accessible and usable when needed by authorized users

Additional Security Properties

  • Authentication: Verifying identity of users, services, or devices
  • Non-repudiation: Ensuring actions cannot be denied

Relationship Management

Purpose

To establish and nurture the links between the organization and its stakeholders at strategic and tactical levels. It includes identification, analysis, monitoring, and continual improvement of relationships with stakeholders.

🎯 Key Activities
  • Stakeholder identification and analysis
  • Building and maintaining relationships
  • Managing stakeholder expectations
  • Facilitating communication
  • Handling complaints and escalations
📊 Success Factors
  • Regular stakeholder engagement
  • Clear communication channels
  • Understanding of business needs
  • Trust and transparency
  • Proactive relationship building
Remember: Relationship management focuses on STRATEGIC relationships, while Service Desk handles day-to-day operational interactions with users.

Supplier Management

Purpose

To ensure that the organization's suppliers and their performance are managed appropriately to support the provision of seamless, quality products and services.

Supplier Evaluation & Selection

  • Define supplier requirements clearly
  • Evaluate based on capability, capacity, and cost
  • Consider strategic value, not just price
  • Assess risks associated with suppliers

Supplier Categorization

Strategic
Senior management involvement, long-term partnerships
Tactical
Significant commercial activity, mid-level management
Operational
Operational products/services, routine management
Commodity
Low-value, easily replaceable, minimal management

IT Asset Management

Purpose

To plan and manage the full lifecycle of all IT assets, to help the organization: maximize value, control costs, manage risks, support decision-making, and meet regulatory and contractual requirements.

IT Asset

Any financially valuable component that can contribute to the delivery of an IT product or service.

Asset Lifecycle

Plan
→
Acquire
→
Deploy
→
Operate
→
Retire
🎯 ITAM vs Service Configuration Management

ITAM: Focuses on financial value and lifecycle of assets.
Service Configuration Management: Focuses on relationships and dependencies between configuration items (CIs).

Monitoring and Event Management

Purpose

To systematically observe services and service components, and record and report selected changes of state identified as events.

Event

Any change of state that has significance for the management of a service or other configuration item (CI).

Types of Events

ℹ️
Informational
No action required, for logging/auditing (e.g., user logged in)
⚠️
Warning
Threshold reached, action may be needed (e.g., disk 80% full)
🚨
Exception
Error or failure, action required (e.g., service crashed)

Release & Deployment Management

Release Management Purpose

To make new and changed services and features available for use.

Release

A version of a service or other configuration item made available for use.

  • Plans release schedule
  • Coordinates release activities
  • Manages release documentation
  • Ensures readiness for deployment
Deployment Management Purpose

To move new or changed hardware, software, or other components to live environments.

Deployment Approaches

  • Phased: Deploy to different groups over time
  • Big Bang: Deploy to all at once
  • Continuous: CI/CD pipeline deployment
  • Pull: Users choose when to update
🎯 Key Distinction

Release Management: WHAT gets released and WHEN (planning)
Deployment Management: HOW it gets moved to production (execution)

The Continual Improvement Model

1. What is the vision?

Business vision, mission, goals, and objectives

2. Where are we now?

Perform baseline assessments

3. Where do we want to be?

Define measurable targets

4. How do we get there?

Define the improvement plan

5. Take action

Execute the improvement actions

6. Did we get there?

Evaluate metrics and KPIs

7. How do we keep the momentum going?

Embed changes and continue improving

Continual Improvement Register (CIR): A database or document used to track and manage improvement ideas from identification through final action.

Value, Outcomes, Costs & Risks

Value

The perceived benefits, usefulness, and importance of something. Determined by stakeholder perception, whether actual or expected.

Outcome

A result for a stakeholder enabled by one or more outputs. Outcomes are what the customer actually wants to achieve.

Cost

The amount of money spent on a specific activity or resource. Services help remove or reduce costs for the consumer.

Risk

A possible event that could cause harm or loss, or make it harder to achieve objectives. Services help remove or reduce risks for the consumer.

Value Formula:
Value = Outcomes Achieved + Costs Removed + Risks Reduced
Utility
The functionality offered by a product or service to meet a need. "What the service DOES" - fit for PURPOSE
Warranty
Assurance that a product or service will meet agreed requirements. "How the service PERFORMS" - fit for USE
