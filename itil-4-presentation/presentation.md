## Slide 3: What is ITIL 4?
*   **Definition:** The most widely accepted approach to IT Service Management (ITSM) in the world.
*   **Evolution:** Moves from "Process-led" (ITIL v3) to "Value-led" (ITIL 4).
*   **Focus:** Co-creation of value, digital transformation, and high-velocity IT.
*   **Key Shift:** Adapts ITSM to modern technologies (Cloud, Agile, DevOps).

---

## Slide 4: Key Concepts of Service Management
*   **Value:** The perceived benefits, usefulness, and importance of something.
*   **Co-creation of Value:** Value is not just delivered; it is co-created by the service provider and the consumer.
*   **Service:** A means of enabling value co-creation by facilitating outcomes that customers want to achieve, without the customer having to manage specific costs and risks.
*   **Service Offerings:** Goods, Access to Resources, Service Actions.

---

## Slide 5: Key Relationships
*   **Service Provider:** The organization providing the service.
*   **Service Consumer:**
    *   **User:** Someone who uses the service.
    *   **Customer:** Someone who defines the requirements and takes responsibility for the outcomes.
    *   **Sponsor:** Someone who authorizes the budget for service consumption.

## Slide 6: The Four Dimensions of Service Management
*   *To ensure a holistic approach, ITIL 4 defines four dimensions that are critical to the effective and efficient facilitation of value for customers and other stakeholders.*
1.  **Organizations and People:** Culture, roles, skills, and responsibilities.
2.  **Information and Technology:** The information and knowledge necessary for the management of services, and the technologies required.
3.  **Partners and Suppliers:** Relationships with other organizations involved in the design, development, deployment, delivery, support, and/or continual improvement of services.
4.  **Value Streams and Processes:** How the various parts of the organization work in an integrated and coordinated way.

## Slide 7: The ITIL Service Value System (SVS)
*   **Purpose:** To ensure that the organization continually co-creates value with all stakeholders through the use and management of products and services.
*   **Components:**
    1.  **Guiding Principles**
    2.  **Governance**
    3.  **Service Value Chain**
    4.  **Practices**
    5.  **Continual Improvement**

---

## Slide 8: The Service Value Chain
*   *The central element of the SVS. An operating model which outlines the key activities required to respond to demand and facilitate value realization.*
*   **Six Key Activities:**
    1.  **Plan:** Shared understanding of vision, status, and improvement direction.
    2.  **Improve:** Continual improvement of products, services, and practices.
    3.  **Engage:** Understanding stakeholder needs and ensuring transparency.
    4.  **Design & Transition:** Ensuring products/services meet expectations.
    5.  **Obtain/Build:** Ensuring service components are available when needed.
    6.  **Deliver & Support:** Ensuring services are delivered and supported according to agreed specifications.

---

## Slide 9: The 7 Guiding Principles
*   *Recommendations that can guide an organization in all circumstances, regardless of changes in its goals, strategies, type of work, or management structure.*
1.  **Focus on Value:** Everything you do must link back to value.
2.  **Start Where You Are:** Don't reinvent the wheel; leverage what exists.
3.  **Progress Iteratively with Feedback:** Do not attempt to do everything at once.
4.  **Collaborate and Promote Visibility:** Work together across boundaries.
5.  **Think and Work Holistically:** No service, or element used to provide a service, stands alone.
6.  **Keep it Simple and Practical:** Eliminate waste; use the minimum number of steps.
7.  **Optimize and Automate:** Human intervention should only happen where it really contributes value.

*   **General Management Practices:** (e.g., Strategy Management, Relationship Management, Supplier Management, Continual Improvement).
*   **Service Management Practices:** (e.g., Service Desk, Incident Management, Problem Management, Change Enablement, Service Level Management).
*   **Technical Management Practices:** (e.g., Deployment Management, Infrastructure and Platform Management, Software Development and Management).

---

## Slide 11: Deep Dive: Key Practices (1/2)
*   **Continual Improvement:** Aligns the organization's practices and services with changing business needs. Uses the *Continual Improvement Model* (What is the vision? -> Where are we now? -> Where do we want to be? -> How do we get there? -> Take action -> Did we get there? -> How do we keep the momentum?).
*   **Change Enablement:** Maximizes the number of successful service and product changes by ensuring that risks have been properly assessed, authorizing changes to proceed, and managing the change schedule. (Note: It's "Enablement", not just "Control").

---

## Slide 12: Deep Dive: Key Practices (2/2)
*   **Incident Management:** Purpose is to minimize the negative impact of incidents by restoring normal service operation as quickly as possible.
*   **Problem Management:** Purpose is to reduce the likelihood and impact of incidents by identifying actual and potential causes of incidents, and managing workarounds and known errors.
*   **Service Desk:** The entry point and single point of contact for the service provider with all of its users.
